Lanterns

The lamplighter came at dusk, as he always did, and the harbour went gold one window at a time. Meera counted them from the sea wall — eleven, twelve — and waited for the thirteenth that never came.

Her grandmother had told her the house on the point kept its lights for the boats still out. “And when it doesn’t?” she had asked. The old woman only folded the map along its tired creases.

Tonight the point was dark
